Reading Nahuatl names
  • x = "sh" (Xolotl = SHOH-lohtl)
  • hu, uh = "w" (Ahuitzotl = ah-WEET-sohtl)
  • qu before e/i = "k"; cu = "kw"
  • z, tz = "s", "ts"
  • tl = one sound, a soft "t" with an "l"
  • Stress the second-to-last syllable
  • -tzin = respectful ending ("dear, honored")
  • -tecuhtli = lord; -cihuatl = lady

The Five Suns: the four ages before ours

SUN 1 · 4 JAGUAR

Tezcatlipoca

People: giants

End: eaten by jaguars

SUN 2 · 4 WIND

Quetzalcoatl

People: blown away by hurricanes

End: survivors became monkeys

SUN 3 · 4 RAIN

Tlaloc

People: rained on by fire

End: survivors became turkeys/birds

SUN 4 · 4 WATER

Chalchiuhtlicue

People: drowned in a flood

End: survivors became fish

SUN 5 · 4 MOVEMENT · NOW

Nanahuatzin → Tonatiuh

Made at: Teotihuacan, by sacrifice

End: foretold to end in earthquakes

Rivalry between Tezcatlipoca and Quetzalcoatl drives the early ages. Sources disagree on the order and on which god ruled which Sun, so if Torres orders them differently, go with the book.

Rulers of Tenochtitlan, in order

  • c.1375–1395 Acamapichtli first king, Culhua royal blood
  • c.1396–1417 Huitzilihuitl his son
  • c.1417–1427 Chimalpopoca Huitzilihuitl's son, Tezozomoc's grandson
  • 1427–1440 Itzcoatl Acamapichtli's son by a low-status mother; broke the Tepanecs
  • 1440–1469 Moctezuma I Ilhuicamina, "the elder"
  • 1469–1481 Axayacatl conquered Tlatelolco, 1473
  • 1481–1486 Tizoc Axayacatl's brother; short, weak reign
  • 1486–1502 Ahuitzotl another brother; great expansion
  • 1502–1520 Moctezuma II Xocoyotzin, "the younger"; met Cortés
  • 1520 Cuitlahuac died of smallpox within months
  • 1520–1525 Cuauhtemoc defended the city; hanged by Cortés

Dates before 1427 are approximate; the annals disagree.
